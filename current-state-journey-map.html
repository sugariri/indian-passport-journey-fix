Passport Seva, step by step

Twenty-three actual steps from login to a booked appointment, read against what the applicant is trying to do. Built from the 22 August 2026 audit of the live portal, before any redesign.

Passport Seva asks citizens to supply what the system should supply — its vocabulary, its arithmetic, its verification and its sequencing — and then gives them no way to know whether they got it right.

Five roots

What the citizen is made to supply

Every observation across the twenty-three steps resolves into one of five. They are separate roots, not restatements, because each needs a different design move. The count on the right is how many steps each one touches.

V

Vocabulary they don’t have

The government’s internal taxonomy is exposed as a set of citizen decisions. ECR and ECNR, Tatkaal, Fresh versus Re-issue versus Identity Certificate, 36 versus 60 pages, Annexures A, G and H, Employment Type, the State → Passport Office → PSK/POPSK hierarchy. The applicant is asked to self-classify into categories they have never encountered, using words the screen never defines.

The moveAsk about circumstances. Derive the category. Show the applicant which category they landed in and why.

10steps
L

Labour the system could do itself

Fields the portal already holds, or could compute from a field on the same screen. ECR status follows from Educational Qualification, three inputs above it. Police Station, District and State follow from the PIN code. Name and date of birth sit inside the Aadhaar it collects and validates. The address is on the document the applicant will nominate eight steps later. All of it is typed by hand.

The moveDerive, then ask the applicant to confirm rather than to compose.

4steps
J

Judgment with no feedback

Nothing the applicant decides is ever confirmed back to them. The address is never checked against the proof that is supposed to establish it. The ECR answer is never reconciled with the qualification already declared. Documents are named in a dropdown but never uploaded, matched or validated. There is no point at which the system says you are ready. The first real feedback is a human being at a counter, weeks later, after payment.

The moveCheck continuously, and put one honest readiness checkpoint before the applicant commits.

9steps
C

Commitment before the deciding information

The Passport Office is chosen on the first screen and locked on save — a browser alert says so — while appointment availability for that office only becomes visible after submission and payment. The truth declaration is signed at step 08; the documents that would make it true are chosen at step 09. Payment happens before any verification of anything.

The movePut the information before the decision. Keep reversible whatever does not need to be locked.

6steps
A

Attention the interface should be managing

Mid-application, the screen is still trying to be four things at once: application workspace, portal dashboard, navigation hub and promotional surface. Quick Links, Home, and Logout hold the same visual weight as the form. The interface never communicates you are in the middle of something important. The only root that is purely presentational — the cheapest to fix and the most immediately visible.

The moveWhen someone is completing an application, the screen becomes application-centric.

5steps
How severity is graded

Three ways a step can hurt

Graded by what it costs the applicant, not by how broken the screen is.

Blocks

You cannot get this right without leaving the site for outside help — a forum, an agent, a phone call, a relative who has done it before.

Risks

You can complete it and be wrong, and you will not find out until the counter. The silent failure mode.

Drags

Costs effort and confidence, but you can recover on your own without help.

Column 8, pulled out

What we fix, and what we deliberately don’t

One discipline runs through the whole list: no fix requires a rule to change. ECR stays a legal category, annexures stay, the quota stays. Everything on the left is achievable inside existing policy — which is what makes the Document Advisor such a find. The rules are already encoded. They are simply wearing a bureaucratic interface, and sitting in the wrong part of the site.

Fix Seven moves

  1. Derive the ECR category from circumstances already declared, name the consequence in plain language, and let the applicant override. Root V, L, J
  2. Document readiness with per-document status — digital where DigiLocker already holds it, original plus copy where it does not. Kills the ambiguous parenthetical. Root V, J
  3. Address confidence — derive district, state and police station from the PIN, and answer the question the applicant is actually asking: does this document prove the address I entered? Root L, J
  4. Availability before commitment — show real slot dates across the relevant centres before the Passport Office is chosen, and make the State → RPO → PSK/POPSK hierarchy visible rather than implied. Root C, V
  5. An application workspace — mid-task, the interface stops competing with the task. Root A
  6. A readiness checkpoint before payment, and again on the receipt. The system takes responsibility for saying you are ready. Root J
  7. A first-timer path — collapse Previous Passport, prefill what Aadhaar and the PIN already provide, and explain why each remaining field is asked. Root L, V, A

Leave And why

  • Login, account creation, captcha. Works. Redesigning it demonstrates nothing.
  • The payment gateway itself. We move where payment sits in the sequence, not how it processes.
  • Slot supply — 45 per centre per day. A capacity policy, not a design problem. We can make the constraint legible and honest. We cannot make more slots.
  • Police verification. Already improving on its own: the mPassport Police App is live in 25-plus states and has cut verification to roughly a fortnight. Redesigning it would be claiming credit for someone else’s work.
  • Name-splitting rules and the criminal declarations. Legal text. We gloss it in plain language; we do not rewrite it.
  • Emergency contact. An ordinary form asked in an ordinary way.
  • Tatkaal, minors, re-issue, lost or damaged, name change, ECR-country specifics, diplomatic and official passports. All outside the one flow. Naming them as out of scope is what keeps the flow honest.
  • Anything needing policy change — abolishing ECR, retiring annexures. Interesting, and not a design deliverable.
The question the map was built to answer

Four root problems, or ten symptoms?

Neither. Five roots — and they are not evenly spread. Vocabulary touches ten steps and absence of feedback touches nine, and the two compound: the applicant is asked to use words they do not have, and is never told whether they used them correctly.

The other three are narrower and sharper. Labour shows up wherever the system asks for something sitting three fields above, one document away, or inside an Aadhaar it already validates. Commitment is concentrated in a single structural fault: the Passport Office is locked at step 06 and appointment availability appears at step 21. Attention is the odd one out — purely presentational, cheapest to fix, and the one that carries a before-and-after most visibly.

What the map settles is where to build. Roots do not accumulate gradually; they converge on six shaded rows. Address Details, the lock alert, the truth declaration, Details Verification, and Appointment Availability — four consecutive screens plus the alert between them — carry every one of the five. That run is the redesign, and everything else is context around it.

The strongest thing this map produces is not a list of faults. It is a single claim with twenty-three pieces of evidence behind it: the system already knows almost everything it asks the applicant to work out. Not a rebuild. A reversal of who does the work.

Honest gaps in the evidence column

  • The 69.26% figure is a share of grievances, not a failure rate. It establishes that this flow is the most-complained-about service in its ministry. It does not establish why.
  • No official rejection-rate data for address mismatch exists in indexed sources. The supporting material is almost entirely passport-agent and insurance SEO content, which is commercially motivated and was discarded. Severity on step 12 is reasoned, not measured — and marked as such.
  • Rajya Sabha Unstarred Question No. 2792, answered 19 December 2024, “Passport Statistics and Reforms” — a primary source not yet read. The most likely place for real numbers.
  • Nine rows rest on design inference rather than external evidence. That is acceptable for a current-state map. It would not be acceptable as a claim about outcomes.
  • No applicant has been interviewed. Every “what they have to understand” cell is inferred from the screen, not observed in use.
