Passport Seva redesign · IA rationale

Every move in the flow, and the failure it fixes

This is an application-readiness redesign for first-time applicants: an adult applying in India for their first ordinary passport through the Normal route, who understands their own circumstances but not passport terminology, evidence rules, or office hierarchy. The single problem: they can complete the current form without knowing whether their category, address evidence, documents and appointment plan are actually ready. The map below takes each surface of the current journey — as evidenced in the screenshot audit and official sources — and states what the redesign does with it and why. No surface moves without a named failure behind it. This document is the implementation baseline, not an immutable source of truth — official evidence, usability testing or a broken prototype interaction may revise it, with every change appended to the decision log rather than silently replacing prior reasoning. With the benchmark set closed, the architecture is frozen: everything from here is solved through content, components or state design — not another restructuring.

Root causes (from the audit)

  • RC1Vocabulary — government terms arrive before the user has context to understand them.
  • RC2Labour — the applicant performs reasoning the service could do itself.
  • RC3Judgment — feedback confirms fields are filled, never that the applicant is ready.
  • RC4Commitment before information — consequences appear after the consequential choice.
  • RC5Attention — portal chrome and quick links compete with the active task.

Scope

One journey, seven recovery paths

The one complete flow

Adult applicant in India · first ordinary passport · Normal route

Entry → completed application → mock payment and appointment → Ready summary. This is the only route built end-to-end. Re-issue, Tatkaal, applying abroad, diplomatic/official, Identity Certificate and minor applicants stay visible but explicitly handed off — building them superficially would weaken the one journey that must be deep.

The portal measures form completion. This journey measures whether a first-time applicant becomes submission-ready and appointment-ready without interpreting passport terminology themselves.

Branches→

Resilience paths inside the same journey — not separate flows

  • Eligibility uncertainty — “not sure” is a valid answer and produces guidance, never a forced guess (Passport options)
  • Address-proof problem — proof doesn’t appear to support the entered address; correct the address or pick another document before submission (Address & office)
  • Document readiness — some documents mock-shared via DigiLocker, others still need preparation or review (Documents)
  • Save and resume — leave to find information, return to the exact stage (everywhere)
  • Application correction — a mistake caught in Review is edited before the declaration (Review & submit)
  • Appointment planning — compare eligible centres and indicative dates before mock payment (Appointment)
  • Honest failure — failed save and failed mock-payment states, surfaced as Saving… / Saved / Couldn’t save — retrying, never hidden (everywhere)
Acceptance criteria, not roadmap: the competition brief makes mobile responsiveness, slower-connection resilience, keyboard and visible-focus support, plain-language guidance, lightweight assets and interruption-safe autosave prototype requirements. Full multilingual coverage may remain future work, but layout and content must be ready for Indian-language translations.
Claim discipline: less Googling, fewer preventable mistakes, fewer repeat visits are hypotheses until tested with users — the case study presents them as expected outcomes, never proven results.
Benchmarks

The reference set is closed

Six references, one job each

Benchmarks govern behaviour, not branding

Passport Seva — policy and operational truth: required information, categories, supporting-document rules, DigiLocker behaviour, RPO/PSK jurisdiction, and the submission → payment → appointment order. We improve its presentation and continuity, never its rules.

UX4G (the Government of India’s own design system) — the primary interaction baseline: eligibility guidance, progress, validation, document upload, save & resume, acknowledgement, payment-failure recovery, My Applications, tracking, language switching and assisted-service handoffs. Built for Indian government services, including accessibility and limited-bandwidth conditions. shadcn may implement the interface; UX4G governs its behaviour — ahead of shadcn, IBM or any foreign visual system.

GOV.UK — content and review patterns: before-you-start information, one important decision at a time, task grouping, plain-language questions, check-answers summaries with an edit link beside every answer, clear confirmation pages. Difficult decisions get focused screens; related factual fields stay grouped — not one field per page.

New Zealand Passports — preparation and continuity: “what you need” as a first-class destination, continue/check saved applications, actionable validation feedback. No photo stage is copied — Indian ordinary-passport applicants are photographed at the PSK; the reusable idea is real-time quality feedback for uploaded evidence.

Ireland Passport Online — concision: one focused transaction, consistent across phone, tablet and desktop, requirements and tracking beside the application, minimal portal navigation. No “ten-minute” target — the Indian first-time journey is more complex; the measure is successful readiness, not speed.

Joined online and in-person service (GOV.UK channel standard) — the product does not end at Submit; the real outcome is successfully attending the PSK. The digital and physical journey connect through a printable/shareable appointment checklist, document-by-document status, centre and appointment information, correction guidance, what happens at the PSK, a human-help route and tracking after submission.

Locks→

What this locks inside the nine stages — no stage added, none reordered

  • Entry — route discovery plus “what you’ll need”
  • Forms — inline validation and honest autosave
  • Documents — checklist, status and actionable quality warnings
  • Review — answers, readiness, edit links, warnings separated from blockers
  • Payment — pending, failed, retry and successful states
  • Appointment — indicative availability vs confirmed booking
  • Ready — reference, centre, carry list, correction help, next steps
  • My Applications — exact resume point and current status
  • Help — contextual guidance plus an assisted route
  • System-wide — responsive, keyboard accessible, lightweight, language-ready
Deliberately rejected: foreign passport policies; foreign identity-referee or photo requirements; generic fintech/KYC dashboards; marketing-style SaaS visuals; AI deciding legal eligibility; “verified”, “approved” or “guaranteed” claims; a separate inferior experience for people who need assistance; adding stages because another country uses them.
Freeze conditions: a structural change now requires official evidence that contradicts the architecture, a real failure in user testing, or an end-to-end interaction that cannot be completed — recorded in the decision log. Everything else is content, component or state design.

Guardrail

Readiness must never become false assurance

Never say

Claims the prototype cannot make

“Your address is verified.” · “Your documents are approved.” · “You are officially eligible.” · “Your appointment is guaranteed.”

Every one of these asserts an official determination that only the PSK and the passport office can make.

Instead→

Preparation states

Confidence about preparation, not verdicts about outcomes

“Ready to review.” · “Information complete.” · “Supporting document selected.” · “Needs action before submission.” · “Official verification happens at the PSK.”

The final state reads: “Prepared for your appointment based on the information provided. Final document and application verification takes place through the official process.”
